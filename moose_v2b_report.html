Moose population trend from integrated camera-trap and iNaturalist data

Model results, regional structure, and comparison with state agency assessments · moose_v2b, converged 26 August 2026

Summary

We fitted the integrated species distribution model to moose (Alces alces) detections from 26,748 camera deployments and 37,636 iNaturalist records, in two parameterisations: a single national trend and a trend that varies among EPA Level I ecoregions. Both chains converged cleanly, and critically both ran as single continuous chains, so neither is exposed to the chunked-MCMC state restoration defect that invalidated our earlier fleet.

We estimate a national decline of −0.119 per year on the link scale (P(<0) = 0.994). However, the model's own robustness criterion fails: the camera-anchored and iNaturalist-specific trend components do not agree well enough for the decline to be called camera-corroborated. Regional structure is present but weakly resolved. Only one of eight ecoregions — Northern Forests, which carries 925 of roughly 1,650 camera sites — supports a credible regional decline, and that is the single region where our estimate contradicts the agencies.

The robustness criterion fails

The indicator is P(year_beta/year_var > 1) = 0.255 in the national model and 0.241 in the ecoregion model, both well under the 0.5 threshold. The mechanism is visible in the table: the iNaturalist-specific component is indistinguishable from zero, so the ratio is unstable by construction. The defensible statement is therefore a national decline of about −0.12 per year driven by the camera-anchored component, with the robustness criterion unmet — not a camera-corroborated decline.

The ecoregion parameterisation agrees closely on the global terms (total −0.1177, camera-anchored −0.1168), so the national estimate does not depend on whether the regional term is included.

Three regions agree, two disagree, and three cannot be assessed. The comparison turns almost entirely on one region. Northern Forests is the only place the model makes a confident claim, and it is the claim that contradicts the agencies: we estimate a decline at P = 0.997 where the dominant agency direction across nine states, covering 87% of the region's area, is stable.

This discrepancy is not necessarily a model failure. Agency designations aggregate across states that may vary internally, our estimand is occupancy and intensity rather than harvestable population, and declines along the southern margin of the boreal forest are well documented. Nevertheless it is a real disagreement in the one place our model speaks clearly, and it should be resolved rather than set aside. The three agreements are weak: in each, the model reports uncertainty and the agency reports stability, which is concordance at the level of "nothing detected" rather than mutual confirmation.

Validation of the re-extraction

The 26 August re-extraction reports 48 missing values for the derived signal-to-noise ratio where the 20 August run reported none. This is a guard, not a dropped subset. The ratio's denominator is the iNaturalist-specific trend, whose 95% CI spans zero, so a minority of posterior draws have a near-zero denominator and the ratio is undefined. The two runs' estimates of that component differ by 0.005, far inside its posterior standard deviation of 0.100, and all other parameters reproduce closely. The re-extraction validates the earlier numbers.

The same condition explains the failed robustness criterion, and it argues for reporting the indicator, which is a probability and always defined, in preference to the raw ratio.

Recommended next step

The disagreement in Northern Forests deserves a targeted analysis before it goes to the wider team. Our estimates are at cell resolution and the agency assessments are state-level, so we can ask directly whether the modelled decline concentrates in the states whose agencies do report declines. If it does, the two sources reconcile at sub-region scale and the apparent conflict is an artifact of aggregating agency calls to the ecoregion.
